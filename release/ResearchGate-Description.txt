Adversarial Structural Security Intelligence (ASSI) — v0.1.0 Research Foundation

Cryptographic validity is not structural security. Adversarial Structural Security Intelligence (ASSI) is an open, specification-first research framework for examining how compositional identities, incomplete or correlated evidence, shifting trust boundaries, adaptive adversaries, and evolving systems can create security failures even when individual cryptographic checks are valid. Motivated by cryptocurrency trust, custody, signing, and liquidity scenarios, the project develops structural security concepts using UTN-based compositional identity, REN directional weak-evidence navigation, Bidirectional Conditional Evidence Feedback (BCEF), independently governed authorization and safety control planes, and Red/Blue/Evaluation/Governance adversarial research roles. It further explores how security residuals can inform Autonomous Questioning Structural Intelligence (AQSI), independently validated recursive self-improvement (RSI), and carefully bounded cross-domain structural transfer. The v0.1.0 research foundation comprises 12 core papers, 6 specification drafts, 5 illustrative cases, and 4 research extensions, accompanied by open questions, 30 testable hypotheses, and a staged validation roadmap. Its proposed four benchmark families address compositional identity, evidence revision, bounded authorization, and adversarial relay. This release presents research hypotheses, contracts, and experimental plans—not validated benchmark results, an operational security product, or a security certification. Inference does not confer authority; simulation does not constitute validation; validation does not imply deployment approval. The project welcomes critical examination, independent replication, negative findings, and collective structural learning.
